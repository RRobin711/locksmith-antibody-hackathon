# Pre-registration — how does yield scale with sequences per backbone?

**2026-09-23, written before any sweep fold.** Nothing below has been run.

## The question, and why it is now the highest-value experiment available

The pilot drew **~3 sequences per backbone** and produced **1 marginal design** (ipSAE
0.637, 3/5 viable, two unrepairable liabilities). Drawing **8** on the same 18 backbones
produced **0.859 on a backbone that had read 0.011**, and took 2 backbones to viability
where 1 of 30 sequences had cleared.

That is two points on a curve. **This measures the curve.** If yield keeps climbing with
depth, backbone diversity was never the limit and the rental is unnecessary. If it
plateaus near where we are, that is the measured argument for renting — rather than an
assumed one.

It is also the transferable result: *how does de novo antibody yield scale with sequences
sampled per backbone?* Nobody had to rent anything to answer it.

## Design

**18 backbones × 32 sequences**, generated by the constrained loop (free liability screen
inside the loop, so every survivor is §9.2-clean by construction). Sequence generation is
CPU and costs ~8 s per 8 draws per backbone — **~10 minutes total for all 576**.

Folding is the cost. To read a *curve* rather than a single point, sequences are folded in
**depth strata**, all 18 backbones at each stratum before moving to the next:

| stratum | sequences/backbone | new folds | this stratum | cumulative new folds | cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| already done | 1 | 18 | — | — | — |
| A | +3 (→4) | 54 | 2.6 h | 54 | **2.6 h** |
| B | +4 (→8) | 72 | 3.5 h | 126 | **6.1 h** |
| C | +8 (→16) | 144 | 7.0 h | 270 | **13.1 h** |
| D | +16 (→32) | 288 | 13.9 h | 558 | **27.0 h** |

At the measured **2.9 min/fold** on the local 5070 Ti.

*(Corrected 2026-09-23: an earlier version of this table gave cumulative 5.1 / 12.0 / 24.0
hours and described A+B as "~4.5 hours". Those were arithmetic errors — the cumulative
column had been written without multiplying through. A+B is **6.1 hours**, not 4.5, and
the full sweep is **27 hours**, not 24. Caught while costing the rented-GPU alternative.
This project has a standing rule about unmeasured projections justifying schedules; this
was worse, being a measured rate multiplied wrongly.)*

**Strata A+B are ~6.1 hours and give three points on the curve (1, 4, 8);** C and D are
overnight commitments and are **not** authorised by this pre-registration — they are run
only if A+B show yield still climbing.

**Order is interruption-survivable by construction:** completing any stratum yields a
usable curve point across all 18 backbones. An interruption mid-stratum loses at most the
folds in flight, and resume keys on artefacts.

## Pre-registered readouts

At each stratum, over the *cumulative* sequence pool per backbone:

1. **How many designs clear ipSAE ≥ 0.60 on the median of five.**
2. **How many reach 5-of-5 viability** — the stricter bar `bb_8_0` meets.
3. **Best design found so far**, and which backbone.
4. **Backbones with at least one viable design** — the quantity that says whether depth
   rescues backbones that looked dead.
5. **Yield per sequence folded**, so the curve is normalised by cost rather than by depth.

## Decision rule, fixed now

Comparing stratum B (8/backbone) against stratum A (4/backbone):

- **Yield still climbing** — more clearing designs *and* more backbones represented —
  → depth is not exhausted. Authorise stratum C, and **do not rent**.
- **Yield flat or falling**, with the best design no better than `bb_8_0`'s 0.859
  → depth is exhausted at this scale. **That is the measured argument for renting**, and
  the campaign plan's backbone-generation arm becomes the next step.
- **Yield climbing but only on backbones already represented** → the pool has a few good
  backbones and many dead ones. Neither depth nor breadth alone is the answer; the right
  move is more depth on the *good* backbones, which is still free.

## What this cannot answer

**Whether any of these bind.** Every readout is ipSAE, which is Boltz's confidence. The
40-crystal panel gives that confidence a known error profile (ρ = +0.702 against pose
accuracy, 0% false-positive, 25% false-negative), which is why the numbers are worth
ranking on — but nothing here is an affinity measurement.

**Statistical resolution.** At 18 backbones a difference of one or two clearing designs
between strata is inside the noise of a 5-sample median. The curve is read as a *shape*
over three or four points, not as a significance test between adjacent strata, and no
p-value will be quoted for the comparison.

**A confound to state now:** deeper sampling is also more selection — keeping the best of
32 is a harder argmax than the best of 4. The per-backbone readouts use the **median of
five diffusion samples** for each sequence, which controls the *diffusion* argmax but not
the *sequence-selection* one. So a rising curve partly reflects selection over a larger
pool, which is exactly what a real campaign would also do; it is not a bias to correct,
but it is a reason the curve's slope overstates what any single extra sequence buys.

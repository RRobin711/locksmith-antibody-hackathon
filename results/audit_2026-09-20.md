# Audit of the 2026-09-20 overnight results

Written the same morning, in response to "are the results to be trusted?". Four checks
were run against the night's own conclusions. **Three of the four found a problem**, and
in each case the problem was in how the result was *stated*, not in the folds.

The folds themselves are sound: 404 folds, zero failures, artefact-asserted per label,
all four experiments pre-registered before their run directories existed.

---

## Check A — is the pLDDT↔spread correlation inflated by construction? **Yes, by ~a third.**

`iface_plddt` and `loop_plddt` are read from the **seed-1** fold. With k=2 the spread is
the deviation between seed 1 and seed 2. So if seed 1 happens to be an odd draw, its
pLDDT is low *and* its deviation from seed 2 is large — the correlation is partly
manufactured by the two quantities sharing a structure.

Disjoint test on the 84 designs with k≥3: take pLDDT from **seed 1** and spread from
**seeds 2 and 3 only**, so no structure is on both sides.

| estimate | ρ | p | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| headline, shared seed 1 (all designs) | **−0.432** | 2.9e−12 | 239 |
| same 84 designs, shared seed 1 | −0.351 | 0.0011 | 84 |
| **same 84 designs, disjoint seeds** | **−0.233** | **0.033** | 84 |

**The effect is real but roughly a third smaller than the headline.** −0.432 should not
be quoted; the defensible number is ≈ **−0.23 to −0.35**, and the disjoint estimate is
itself attenuated (it rests on a single pair), so the truth is probably near the top of
that range.

### What this does to the "H1 refuted" claim — it was overstated

The 2026-09-18 claim was ρ −0.168 (p=0.69) at **n=8**. The clean estimate here is
**−0.233**. Those two numbers are statistically indistinguishable: an n=8 correlation has
a 95% CI roughly ±0.7 wide.

So the earlier finding was **underpowered, not wrong**. Calling it "refuted" and
"noise read as an effect" was too strong and is withdrawn. The accurate statement:

> Mean loop pLDDT has a **weak but real** negative association with CDR-H3 spread
> (ρ ≈ −0.23, p = 0.033, n = 84 disjoint). The 2026-09-18 conclusion that it is
> *uncorrelated* is not supported — there is an effect — but the effect is small, and
> the dramatic contrast reported this morning was inflated by the estimator.

`results/ensemble_wide.md` reports the shared-seed figures and must be read with this
correction; the same applies to every disattenuated value in its table.

## Check B — did the aromatic result "reverse"? **No. It replicates almost exactly. I said the wrong thing.**

This morning's summary said "the aromatic-fraction result reverses". That was wrong, and
it came from testing a different hypothesis than the one 2026-09-18 actually claimed.
The original was about predicting **DockQ**. The overnight partial correlations were
about predicting **spread**. Different outcome variable, so they were never comparable.

Re-run on the T=0.1 arm, which is the arm the original was measured on:

| quantity | 2026-09-18 (n=40) | tonight, T=0.1 (n=60) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| aromatics → DockQ, raw | −0.536 | **−0.535** | replicates exactly |
| aromatics → DockQ, controlling spread | −0.410 | **−0.408** | replicates exactly |
| aromatics → spread, raw | +0.621 | +0.455 | weaker, same sign |
| **spread → DockQ, controlling aromatics** | **−0.081 (p=0.62)** | **−0.303** | **does NOT replicate** |

And over all 239 designs: spread → DockQ controlling aromatics = **−0.289 (p=5.7e−06)**;
aromatics → DockQ controlling spread = **−0.381 (p=1.2e−09)**.

**The aromatic filter is vindicated**, not overturned — its correlation reproduced to
within 0.002 on an independent sample of designs. What fails is the *other half* of that
session's conclusion:

> "**Ensemble dropped from selection**" — justified by the partial spread→DockQ of
> **−0.081 (p=0.62)**, i.e. "the costly measurement carried no information the sequence
> didn't already have." At n=239 that partial is **−0.289 (p=5.7e−06)**, and at n=60 on
> the very same arm it is −0.303. **The ensemble axis does carry independent signal.**

That conclusion is withdrawn. It is currently a bullet in `LEARNINGS.md`.

## Check C — the ablation verdict depends on which metric adjudicates, and the code chose one

The generated verdict said "controls degrade about as much as hotspots → uninformative".
That branch was decided on **contacts**. Per metric:

| metric | hotspots mean Δ | controls mean Δ | ratio | triple (158 contacts removed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DockQ** | **−0.038** | −0.001 | **34×** | −0.030 |
| ipSAE | −0.025 | −0.004 | 5.7× | −0.021 |
| ΔG | +0.146 | +0.237 | 0.6× | −0.012 |
| contacts | −0.6 | −2.1 | 0.3× | **+3.9** |

On DockQ and ipSAE hotspots degrade far more than controls; on ΔG and contacts the
controls move more. The prereg's H1 named "ΔG and contacts" and on those it **fails**;
H2 (controls within 3 seed sd) **passes** on every metric.

**"Uninformative" survives, but for a different reason than the code gave.** It is not
that controls degrade as much — that is true only on two of four metrics. It is that
**only 1 of 9 mutants (R97A, ΔDockQ −0.126) exceeds 3 seed sd on anything.** Everything
else is inside the noise floor.

What does survive unqualified is the triple: **removing 158 antigen heavy-atom contacts,
including R100's 81, costs 0.030 DockQ (under 2 seed sd) and *gains* 3.9 contacts.**
Boltz re-docks the mutant and rebuilds an interface that scores as well as the original.
That is a statement about the predictor and it is not metric-dependent.

## Check D — the TIM-3 specificity result: non-overlapping, but **p = 0.100, which is the floor**

| | ipSAE per seed | mean | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| pembrolizumab | 0.322, 0.189, 0.458 | 0.323 | 0.269 wide |
| our design | 0.624, 0.612, 0.469 | 0.568 | |
| 8TBB Fab (real binder) | 0.614, 0.747, 0.684 | 0.682 | |

The ranges do not overlap, which looks decisive. It is not: **with 3 vs 3 the smallest
attainable two-sided Mann–Whitney p is exactly 0.100**, and 0.100 is what we measured.
The test was at its ceiling before it began.

Note also pembrolizumab's spread — 0.189 to 0.458 — is an order of magnitude wider than
the ~0.02 single-seed ipSAE sd on the cognate PD-1 complex. Against a non-cognate antigen
the predictor is much less stable, so both means are poorly determined at n=3.

**This morning's phrasing — "the design *is* TIM-3-reactive", "the escape is closed" —
overstates it.** The supported claim is: *suggestive, non-overlapping at n=3, p=0.100,
not established.* `scripts/47_tim3_more_seeds.py` is folding 5 more seeds per arm
(n=8 vs 8, where the attainable p floor is 1.6e−4) to settle it.

## What this means about the earlier sessions

**Yes, there is an earlier mistake, and it is one mistake made twice.**

Both the "loop pLDDT is blind" claim (n=8) and the "ensemble carries no independent
information" claim (partial −0.081, n=40, one temperature arm) are **small-sample nulls
promoted to rules**. In both cases a wide confidence interval around zero was read as
evidence of absence, and in both cases a larger sample shows a real, modest effect.

This project already knows this failure mode — `LEARNINGS.md` carries "measure the noise
floor before comparing any correlation to a threshold" and the Fisher-z arithmetic
showing that n=14 cannot adjudicate a 0.6 gate. The rule was applied to *positive*
findings and not to *nulls*. A null needs its detectable-effect bound stated with the
same discipline: **"no effect" is only meaningful as "no effect larger than x".**

The practical damage is limited — the aromatic filter, which is what actually got used,
replicated almost exactly — but the reasoning that dropped the ensemble axis from
selection was wrong, and two `LEARNINGS.md` entries need amending.

## Check D, settled at n=8 (added after folding 5 more seeds per arm)

| antibody vs TIM-3 | n | mean ipSAE | sd | range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pembrolizumab (certain non-binder) | 8 | **0.331** | 0.187 | 0.015 – 0.641 |
| **our design** | 8 | **0.513** | 0.100 | 0.335 – 0.624 |
| 8TBB Fab (real TIM-3 binder) | 3 | **0.682** | 0.066 | 0.614 – 0.747 |

- ours vs pembrolizumab: U=52, **p = 0.038**
- ours vs the real binder: U=1, **p = 0.024**

**The design sits significantly above a specific antibody and significantly below a real
binder** — intermediate, +0.182 over pembrolizumab and −0.169 under the true binder. So
there is a modest, statistically detectable TIM-3 signal. It is not the near-binder
picture the first three seeds painted: our mean fell from **0.568 (n=3) to 0.513 (n=8)**,
i.e. the original triple was a favourable draw, which is the winner's curse operating on
a three-sample mean.

Two operational lessons, both quantified here:

1. **ipSAE against a non-cognate antigen is wildly unstable** — pembrolizumab spans
   0.015 to 0.641 across eight seeds (sd 0.187) against ~0.02 on its cognate complex.
   Any single-seed specificity screen is worthless, and three seeds is marginal.
2. **The n=3 result was at the arithmetic floor of its test** (p=0.100 is the smallest
   attainable value for 3 vs 3) and was reported as though it were decisive. Check the
   attainable p of a design before reading its output.

## Construct validity — the question the project never asked

The checks above are about how results were stated. This one is about what was measured,
and it is the more serious finding.

Take the 09-17 calibration panel's substitution ladder (Fab DockQ against the 5GGS
crystal, pembrolizumab's CDR-H3 mutated 0 → 13 positions):

| substitutions | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 13 | poly-Gly |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CDR-H3 identity | 100 | 92.3 | 84.6 | 76.9 | 69.2 | 53.8 | 38.5 | 23.1 | 0 | — |
| Fab DockQ | 0.817 | 0.818 | 0.751 | 0.777 | 0.744 | 0.727 | 0.604 | 0.570 | 0.512 | **0.036** |

Regressing DockQ on CDR-H3 identity over that range: **R² = 0.95**, slope 0.0032 DockQ
per percentage point of identity, Spearman +0.967.

**In a fixed-backbone redesign scored against the parent's own crystal, DockQ is to first
order a measure of how little you changed the parent.** The rubric weights binding at
60%, DockQ is one of its six members, and it then weights novelty — *dissimilarity* of
the same loop — at 20%. The objective contains a quantity and its near-inverse.

Two things stop this being fatal, and both need saying:

- **The predictor is genuinely sequence-responsive.** Poly-Gly collapses DockQ to 0.036,
  and full CDR-H3 replacement gives 0.512. This is not a model returning the memorised
  complex regardless of input, and an earlier draft of this audit that suggested so was
  wrong.
- **Within the M3 pool there is real residual signal.** Identity spans only 15.4–46.2%
  there, and explains just **R² = 0.149** of DockQ variance; pool DockQ sd is 0.0392
  against a single-seed noise sd of 0.018, implying between-design reliability **0.789**.
  So designs do differ in a reproducible way beyond their identity.

What is *not* established, at any point in seven days, is that the residual signal tracks
anything a laboratory would recognise. DockQ measures similarity of a predicted complex
to the parent's crystal; ipSAE measures the predictor's self-consistency; PRODIGY ΔG is a
contact-count regression over the predicted pose. All three are downstream of one
structure from one model whose measured median DockQ on *novel* antibody–antigen pairs is
**0.291**. The defensible claim is:

> Our pipeline produces CDR-redesigned variants that a structure predictor scores as
> retaining pembrolizumab's binding mode, with reproducible design-to-design differences
> of unknown physical meaning.

Not "we designed an antibody", and not "these designs bind PD-1".

## Check F (added later the same day) — the NetSolP numbers in this very audit were wrong

The sections above report NetSolP as "VH 0.5617-0.6187, VL 0.6257, shortfall 0.0813".
An independent audit against the handbook then found that every scoring call fed
NetSolP the **full Fab chains**, where the handbook specifies the **Fv (VH + VL)**,
twice. Re-measured on the correct input, over all 239 designs:

| | Fab (what was scored) | **Fv (what the handbook specifies)** |
|---|---|---|
| pembrolizumab VH | 0.623 | **0.733** |
| pembrolizumab VL | 0.626 | **0.569** |
| designs, VH range | 0.5617 – 0.6187 | **0.6677 – 0.7424** (mean 0.7061) |
| designs with VH in the Good band (>= 0.70) | 0 | **168 / 239 (70%)** |
| designs beating the parent's own heavy chain | 0 | **4** |
| which chain limits `min()` | heavy | **light** |
| shortfall to Good | 0.0813 | **0.1314** |

**The conclusion survives and its explanation inverts.** 0/239 still reach the Good
band, so developability is still pinned at Medium -- but not because CDR redesign
makes the antibody less soluble. On the correct input **70% of the designs have a
heavy chain in the Good band**, and the composite is held down entirely by the one
chain ProteinMPNN never touches. Under `chain_agg: min` the fixed light chain at
0.5686 sets the score for every design that could ever be made this way.

Two things follow:

1. **There is a concrete lever, and this audit previously said there was none.**
   Redesigning the light-chain CDRs would move developability Good and `final` from
   87.5 to 92.5. "20% of the rubric is an unreachable ceiling" was the wrong reading;
   "20% of the rubric is gated by a chain we never designed" is the right one.
2. **The wrong input pointed at the wrong chain.** On Fab the heavy chain looked
   limiting (0.623 vs 0.626) and the natural inference was that redesign had hurt
   solubility. On Fv the ordering reverses. A convention error does not merely shift
   a number; it can invert the causal story you tell about it.

Also superseded: any statement that ProteinMPNN "degraded developability in 239/239
cases". That was computed on Fab chains. On the Fv the designs' mean VH (0.7061) sits
just below the parent's (0.7328) and four designs exceed it.

## Check G — a discrepancy I reported that does not exist

Earlier today I reported that ipSAE computed from our AlphaFold-style JSON (0.8491)
differed from the Boltz `.npz` route (0.8560), and attributed it to a code-path
difference in `ipsae.py`. **That is wrong and is withdrawn.**

Both routes on the same structure agree to five decimals (A–C: 0.823561 vs 0.823574; the
residual is the writer's 2-dp PAE rounding). The 0.8560 was the **8-seed mean** in
`results/m3_winner.md`; seed 1 alone is 0.8491, which the JSON reproduces exactly.

Fourth overstatement of the day, fourth toward a more interesting finding. The
consequence is benign — the PAE converter is sound and the packaged submission's ipSAE is
the same number our own pipeline computes — but the error was in comparing an aggregate
to a single observation without checking which was which.
